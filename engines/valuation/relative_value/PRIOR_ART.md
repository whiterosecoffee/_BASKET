# Prior art that informs the rotation engine

Citations are from memory of well-known literature. Verify before relying on a specific claim or number. Nothing here is a validated edge: the engine reports facts and scenario arithmetic, and these families say where it is likely to be wrong.

| # | Family | Core idea (key refs) | What the engine does now | Gap / improvement | Data needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Relative valuation and comps | Multiples are only comparable after normalising growth, risk, accounting and calendar. PEG (Lynch); regression comps of P/E on growth; Damodaran's relative-valuation work | Calendarized NTM P/E 2026-30, PEG, cohort P/E~growth residual | Add EV/EBITDA and EV/FCF residuals, risk (beta, leverage) as regressors, robust regression (n is small), sector-specific multiples | ratios, statements (have) |
| 2 | Earnings momentum and revisions | Post-earnings-announcement drift (Ball & Brown 1968; Bernard & Thomas 1989/90), standardized unexpected earnings, revision momentum (Givoly & Lakonishok 1979; Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok 1996) | Not yet. `estimates.csv` is append-only with `as_of`, so revisions are already recoverable | Revision breadth and 30/90-day EPS-revision %, SUE from actual vs estimate, guidance delta; rank candidates on revisions, not level | Estimate history (must accumulate or buy) |
| 3 | Pairs / statistical arbitrage | Relative price mean-reverts when anchored by common fundamentals: distance method (Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst 2006), cointegration (Engle-Granger; Vidyamurthy 2004), OU half-life | Price-ratio z-score, percentile, AR(1) half-life | Anchor the spread to the *fundamental* ratio (EPS-implied) not history alone; cointegration/ADF test; refuse signals when the ratio is non-stationary (regime break) | prices (have) |
| 4 | Multi-factor / quality | Value, profitability, investment, quality (Fama-French; Novy-Marx 2013 gross profitability; Piotroski 2000 F-score; Asness et al. quality) | Not yet | Add gross profitability, accruals, leverage, F-score as a quality guard so cheap-because-broken names are not rotated *into* | statements (have) |
| 5 | Expectations investing | Read what the price implies, then ask whether it is too high or low (Mauboussin & Rappaport; reverse DCF) | Not yet. Cleanest test of "market already priced the re-rating" | ratios, cash flow (have) |
| 6 | Consensus bias and guidance behaviour | Analysts are optimistic on long-run growth (Chan, Karceski & Lakonishok 2003); firms guide to beatable numbers (Matsumoto 2002); forecast combination beats single sources (Bates & Granger 1969); earnings mean-revert (Fama & French 2000) | Growth fade x0.8/yr on extrapolated years, tagged by `kind` | Track each source's historical error and bias-correct; shrink long-horizon growth toward the cohort/GDP; weight sources by track record | Estimate vs actual history |
| 7 | Event studies and earnings premia | Announcement-window returns and volume premia (Beaver 1968; Frazzini & Lamont 2007); options-implied earnings move | `WAIT_FOR_PRINT` rule from days to report, hurdle 2x | Size the catalyst: implied move vs the stock's own historical earnings move, to decide hold-through vs trim-before; staged entry after the print | options chain (Massive has it; entitlement unchecked), events |
| 8 | Transaction-cost and tax-aware trading | No-trade band around the optimum (Constantinides 1986); dynamic trading with costs (Garleanu & Pedersen 2013); tax-timing option (Constantinides 1983) | Flat round-trip cost and tax on unrealised gain, fixed hurdle | Derive hurdle from volatility and costs; model lots, holding period (short vs long term) and wash-sale rules; include the option value of waiting | positions with lots |
| 9 | Momentum, reversal and liquidity | Intermediate momentum (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993), short-term reversal (Jegadeesh 1990), 52-week-high effect (George & Hwang 2004), high-volume return premium (Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin 2001) | `tape.py`: new-low, near-low, heavy-volume and gap-down flags, shown in notes | Use as risk gating and entry timing, never as value: a name at a 52-week low on 2x volume is neither "cheap" nor "capitulated" without further evidence | prices/volume (have) |
| 10 | Cyclical normalization | Earnings are not a stationary multiple base for cyclicals: Shiller CAPE (Campbell & Shiller 1988), mid-cycle margins | Not yet | Memory and capex-cycle names (e.g. SNDK trailing P/E 4.6x) need mid-cycle EPS/margin, not peak-year EPS; flag low P/E as cycle-peak risk | 5+ yr statements (have) |

## What today's tape adds (NVDA, CRWV, SNDK, 2026-11-20)
All three gapped down 12-16% in one session and are down 22-33% over ten trading days. NVDA and CRWV printed new 52-week lows, NVDA on 2.4x its prior-20-day volume. Families 9 and 10 apply directly: a low trailing multiple (SNDK 4.6x, NVDA 15.7x) is weak evidence in a cycle-sensitive group, and the first lever to add is the revisions family (2), because an earnings reset shows up there before it shows up in price.

## Priority order
1. **Revisions + SUE** (family 2): the structure already supports it; needs only repeated estimate snapshots.
2. **Reverse DCF** (5) and **cyclical normalization** (10): buildable now from Massive statements and ratios.
3. **Catalyst sizing** from options (7) if the options entitlement exists.
4. **Quality guard** (4) and **robust residuals** (1).
5. **Signal ledger and out-of-sample test** (11) before any size is attached to a signal.
6. **Lot/wash-sale tax model** (8) once real positions are loaded.
