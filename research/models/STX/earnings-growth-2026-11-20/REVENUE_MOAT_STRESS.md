## TAM translation

Issuer supply roadmap targets nearline exabyte growth around25% compounded. This is company capacity guidance, not independently measured industry TAM. Normalizing starting exabytes to100 implies125/156.25/195.31 after1/2/3 years if achieved. Revenue=exabytes*realized price/TB, with product/mix caveats. At25% volume growth, -10%/0%/+10% price per TB gives12.5%/25%/37.5% revenue growth. More bytes therefore do not by themselves establish vendor revenue forecasts or growing margins. Total generated data is not all retained, purchased annually or addressable by Seagate. No defensible incremental dollar TAM established in this review.

## Moat and integration

March3 issuer release: Mozaic4+ up to44TB shipping in volume to two leading hyperscalers; custom in-house laser/photonics engineering and production know-how. Production qualification and yield control support a difficult-to-replicate capability; economic moat strength is an inference, not a measured permanent monopoly.
https://investors.seagate.com/news/news-details/2026/Seagate-Delivers-Industrys-Highest-Capacity-Hard-Drives-with-Next-Generation-Mozaic-4/default.aspx

Oct26 issuer release: Broadcom HBA approved-vendor-list support for Seagate HAMR+SMR, with Microsoft Azure qualification. Integration reduces adoption friction and can broaden reachable customers; no separately priced software/service royalty or exclusive customer lock-in disclosed.
https://investors.seagate.com/news/news-details/2026/Seagate-Simplifies-High-Capacity-Storage-Scaling-for-AI-and-Cloud-Infrastructure/default.aspx

Competitive check: WD's Feb3 roadmap announced40TB UltraSMR qualification and planned2027 HAMR ramp, plus performance/power/platform initiatives. Those are vendor plans, not verified completed shipments as of this review. They demonstrate competing roadmaps; do not model an uncontested Seagate capacity lead indefinitely.
https://www.westerndigital.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2026-02-03-western-digital-accelerates-storage-innovation-for-ai-era

Stress channels: rival qualification and pricing pressure; concentrated hyperscaler purchasing; delayed customer acceptance; yield/scrap and external component cost; power/site timing; SSD substitution in warmer workloads; cost/TB improvement passed through to customers. Required watchpoints: exabytes, realized price/TB and mix, cost/TB/yield, qualification milestones, gross margin, diluted shares and contract repricing.

## Premium services: critical correction

Wasabi's April9 announcement explicitly states it acquired Lyve Cloud and Seagate received equity in Wasabi; transaction terms otherwise undisclosed. Do not attribute ongoing Lyve Cloud subscription revenue to Seagate or assume consolidation. Stake percentage, value and accounting treatment not established here. No inference about other Lyve assets transferred solely from the Lyve Cloud announcement.
https://wasabi.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/wasabi-technologies-to-acquire-seagate-s-lyve-cloud-business

Rescue recovery services remain marketed; product existence does not establish separate high-margin recurring revenue, and coverage may be bundled with drives. Systems/support/firmware integration can support hardware pricing and customer retention, but attach rates, renewal revenue and standalone margins remain unknown. Assign zero incremental service uplift to this sensitivity until quantified.
https://www.seagate.com/products/rescue-data-recovery/
