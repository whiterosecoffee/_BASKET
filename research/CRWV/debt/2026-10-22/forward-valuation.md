# CRWV forward equity valuation

October 22, 2026. USD. B10 #68 / draft PR #69. Analyst scenarios, not consensus estimates, company guidance, a live quote, or a calibrated probability distribution.

## Conclusion

Conditional central present equity value: approximately **$90–110/share** if 2028 revenue reaches about $34B and operating margin after depreciation and stock compensation reaches 26%, with $4.5B net expensed interest and 650M economically diluted shares. A stronger outcome supports roughly $170–200; slower conversion can produce $15–25 or worse. These ranges are earnings-capitalization sensitivities, not independently verified DCF fair values. The central case needs execution and sufficient funding; it is not an assertion that today's balance sheet already funds it.

## Earnings and valuation scenarios

Formula: EPS = (revenue x operating margin - net expensed interest) x (1 - tax rate) / economic diluted shares. Tax assumption 20%; no exceptional investment gains. Operating margin includes depreciation, recurring overhead and stock compensation; it is not contract EBITDA or management's adjusted margin. The diluted-share assumptions allow for additional capital and equity awards; they are not exact treasury-stock/if-converted accounting forecasts. Interest is kept as an economic expense alongside this dilution allowance, conservatively avoiding an unearned interest add-back. An exact convertible settlement model could alter both.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $28B | $34B | $40B |
| Operating margin after D&A/SBC | 20% | 26% | 30% |
| Operating income | $5.60B | $8.84B | $12.00B |
| Net expensed interest | $4.80B | $4.50B | $4.80B |
| Net income at 20% tax | $0.64B | $3.472B | $5.76B |
| Economic diluted shares | 675M | 650M | 675M |
| EPS | $0.95 | $5.34 | $8.53 |
| Selected terminal P/E | 18–22x | 22–26x | 26–30x |
| End-2028 equity price | $17–21 | $118–139 | $222–256 |
| Present equivalent at 12%, 2.2 years | $13–16 | $92–108 | $173–200 |
| Present equity capitalization | $9–11B | $60–70B | $117–135B |

Cap values use each scenario's future economic diluted shares; they are not today's outstanding-share market capitalization. No debt is subtracted a second time from P/E equity value: its cost is already deducted in earnings. Debt, leases, maintenance/replacement investment and restricted cash still need a full FCFE/DCF cross-check. These P/E multiples are analyst choices conditioned on growth and cash conversion, not asserted current peer multiples. The low case is not a liquidation floor. A delayed/loss-making outcome can invalidate an earnings multiple altogether.

My central valuation band is rounded to $90–110. Strong execution values require stronger margins, sustained growth and better financing visibility; they should not be treated as the central fair value merely because backlog is large. A prolonged funding interruption is outside these going-concern scenarios.

## Why depreciation can change the answer materially

A possible central cost bridge is 65% adjusted EBITDA margin, less 3% recurring stock compensation/other operating adjustments, less 36% D&A = 26% operating margin. This is an assumption, not a forecast reconciliation. At $34B revenue it implies $12.24B annual D&A. That depreciation is roughly a $73B six-year-equivalent asset base before separating other asset categories, age, salvage and retirements. Given rapid capital additions, actual cohort depreciation could be materially larger.

Every additional $1B D&A or interest reduces central EPS by **$1.23**, holding taxes/shares fixed. Increasing modeled D&A to $15B without any associated revenue/cost benefit reduces EPS to about **$1.94**, and the present P/E value to roughly **$33–39**. That is a sensitivity, not a coherent new buildout forecast: incremental assets should ordinarily also bring revenue. It demonstrates why assuming the target operating margin without checking the equipment cohorts can misprice equity.

Conversely, at unchanged $12.24B D&A, extending the same equivalent new asset base from six to eight years would lower its annual allocation by about $3.06B and raise modeled EPS by $3.77 before changing any other assumption. Actual existing-cohort accounting is prospective and depends on age and remaining book value; no blanket company-wide change is forecast. Accounting changes alone do not create cash and should not mechanically earn the same P/E uplift. Evidence of profitable renewals and lower replacement needs matters.

## What 2027 versus 2028 EPS means

2027 should be treated as a transition year. A $24B revenue illustration with 16% operating margin and $4B interest gives approximately -$0.25/share before any assumed loss tax benefit, at 650M shares. At a 22% operating margin, otherwise unchanged, it gives about $1.58/share after 20% tax. Thus near-zero to low-single-digit 2027 earnings can coexist with $4–6 central 2028 EPS if billing and operating leverage catch up. These are scenario examples, not a consensus EPS range.

A realistic mature P/E is about 20–26x if growth remains attractive and cash conversion improves. A 26–30x premium requires more convincing execution and recurring software/services economics. No 40–50x multiple is required for the central case. A high multiple on depressed 2027 EPS can be meaningless; apply it to an explicitly dated earnings base and discount to the present.

## Credit improvement and rate sensitivities

Fitch's July assessment: parent BB-/Positive; DDTL 5.5 BB+. Source: https://www.fitchratings.com/research/corporate-finance/fitch-assigns-coreweave-new-ddtl-bb-rating-affirms-idr-at-bb-outlook-positive-16-07-2026

Analyst mechanism: acceptance/billing removes construction risk; collections establish operating cash; project amortization reduces principal; more customers and renewals improve resilience; new borrowing can then be secured on better evidence. This is a credible path to lower spreads and a lower equity discount rate. It is not continuous or automatic: new funding can exceed debt amortization, while macro benchmark rates or market spreads can rise despite operating progress. Ratings are periodic judgments, not a daily score tied to the backlog balance.

The historical issuer presentation showed effective borrowing cost falling from 14.9% FY2023 to 8.3% Q2 2026. Its preliminary 7.8% pro-forma number is not a verified current cost; see debt audit for final convertible terms.

At 650M shares and 20% tax, every 1-point rate reduction on **$10B actually repriced/refinanced** saves $100M pretax and adds about **$0.123 EPS**. A 2-point reduction on $30B adds about **$0.74 EPS**, or roughly $14/share present value at 24x and the same discount convention. This is not a promise that fixed coupons reset. Refinancing fees, hedges and callable terms can reduce savings. Increased earnings and multiple expansion from lower risk can both occur, but must be modeled coherently.

At $34B revenue, +1 operating-margin point contributes $340M pretax and about **$0.42 EPS**, worth about $8/share present value at 24x. Pricing, deployment efficiency and lower financing cost therefore have comparable, measurable value. Fixed backlog should not all be repriced; higher pricing enters through new contracts, shorter terms and renewals.

## Services, residual equipment and capital structure

Central margins embed the existing software/managed-services strategy; no separate software valuation is added. Stronger services revenue can help reach the upper margins, but the current disclosures do not support valuing the whole business as SaaS. Consulting may grow dollars of profit without raising blended margin.

The same principle applies to older GPU renewals: they can reduce future capital intensity and improve post-debt cash flow, but must not be counted as both a separate residual-value asset and the cash flows already sustaining the P/E. Existing NVIDIA support improves financing/demand context without eliminating the shareholder's funding, delivery or dilution exposure.

## Validation and limitations
